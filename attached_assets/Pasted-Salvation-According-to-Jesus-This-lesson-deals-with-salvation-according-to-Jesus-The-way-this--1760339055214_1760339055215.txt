Salvation According to Jesus
This lesson deals with salvation, according to Jesus.

The way this teaching came about was when I was in college, I had two Jehovah's Witnesses come to my door. One of them had been a deacon in a mainline denomination for 20 years. They began to tell me the Jehovah's Witness version of salvation.

It was simply rhetoric, what they had been brainwashed to teach. I brought up scriptures in the Bible that very solidly countered their teaching. They were taking scriptures out of context and twisting and warping them, it was very irrational and illogical.

I was amazed at the cognitive dissonance these people were capable of; seemingly normal, everyday people. It was a pleasant conversation, the trainee did seem to be affected by it, but the main person was undeterred. They left, promising to come back again. But, it made me think, could I actually be brainwashed into teachings that were off, maybe not that far off, but even a little bit off?

So, I decided to go to the teachings of Jesus, the author of our salvation, and read what he says about salvation. So, I listed all the scriptures where Jesus talks about salvation, started reading them, and I got this jumbled, confusing message. It was contradictory, as one verse would say, "just believe in Jesus and you'll have salvation," and another would say, "give up everything in order to follow me."

I was a bit taken back, so I told God, you're going to have to help me understand this.

I continued studying the verses, and as I read them, the scriptures suddenly seemed to fall into one of three categories: belief, obedience, and total commitment. So, I put the scriptures in each of those categories, and the teachings lined up perfectly.

Of course, we also have repentance. That was the main message of Jesus when he was here on earth. Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.

The categories helped me to make sense of Jesus' teachings in a much deeper way. I had been taught the "Romans Road" to salvation for sharing with people how to be saved. That's really good for belief and repentance. But it doesn't get into obedience, and it definitely does not get into total commitment.

I think a lot of denominations teach belief, repentance, and stop there. They ignore the harder teachings, even though Jesus is clear about them.

Belief

So, let's look at what Jesus taught, starting with belief. It says in John 3:16, the famous scripture, "For God so loved the world that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life" (John 3:16).

Belief is the key ingredient. If you don't believe Jesus is God, then you have missed the first and most vital part of salvation. That's one of the first confessions of the early church: "Iesous ho Kurios" in the Greek, or "Jesus is LORD." This is mentioned in Corinthians, Romans, and Philippians. "Kurios" is translated in the Greek Septuagint to "Yahweh" in Hebrew. So, they are actually saying Jesus is Yahweh; without that, you cannot be saved.

Jesus claims this multiple times. He says, "Before Moses was, I AM." He's calling himself the "I AM," Yahweh.

In John, Jesus says, "I assure you, those who listen to my message and believe in God who sent me have eternal life. They will never be condemned for their sins, but they have already passed from death into life" (John 5:24).

Jesus told her, "I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die like everyone else, will live again" (John 11:25).

So, Jesus refers to himself as the "I AM," Yahweh. Also, the resurrection and the life; only God himself can claim that, no mere man or prophet can.

"But these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (John 20:31).

"Whoever believes and is baptized will be saved, but whoever does not believe will be condemned" (Mark 16:16).

So, we see that refusal to believe leads to condemnation. You cannot have salvation if you do not have belief.

Repentance

And of course, repentance is a basic necessity of salvation. It's what Jesus taught when he was here on earth. That was his message: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the gospel" (Mark 1:15).

"From that time on Jesus began to preach, 'Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near'" (Matthew 4:17).

"So they went out and proclaimed that people should repent" (Mark 6:12). This is referring to the disciples. This was the main message they were given to tell everyone. Repentance is also what John the Baptist preached.

"No, I tell you; but unless you repent, you will all likewise perish" (Luke 13:3). Jesus is talking about the tower of Siloam falling and killing people and the incident where Herod killed some people and mixed their blood with their sacrifice.

The people believed these incidents happened because these victims were especially evil. But Jesus said they're no more evil than anyone else. He is telling them that all people will perish if they don't repent.

"Just so, I tell you, there is joy before the angels of God over one sinner who repents" (Luke 15:10).

So, this is a great source of joy in heaven when even one person repents.

"He told them, 'This is what is written: The Messiah will suffer and rise from the dead on the third day, and repentance for the forgiveness of sins will be preached in his name to all nations, beginning at Jerusalem'" (Luke 24:46-47).

So, you can repent and be forgiven of sins now, because the Messiah suffered and rose from the dead. His perfect sacrifice allowed us to be forgiven with just repentance, instead of the Old Covenant animal sacrifices.

But repentance is required; without it, there is no forgiveness or salvation.

Obedience

The next thing that I saw was obedience. Obedience was taught in some of the churches I had attended, but not emphasized, and very rarely tied to salvation.

Jesus has a different perspective on obedience in his teachings. Without it, your faith is useless, and you are destined to fail.

"Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father who is in heaven" (Matthew 7:21).

If we remember the sheep and the goats, the sheep did, and the goats didn't. It was all about obedience, about doing what Jesus commanded.

"Therefore everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice is like a wise man who built his house on the rock" (Matthew 7:24).

Of course, we know that obeying his teachings puts your house on a firm foundation so that when the storms of life come, your faith will stand. If you don't obey his teachings, you build your house on the sand, and when the storms of life come, your faith will be destroyed.

"For whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother" (Matthew 12:50).

The family of God consists of those who do his will.

“Why do you ask me about what is good?” Jesus replied. “There is only One who is good. If you want to enter life, keep the commandments” (Matthew 19:17).

"Whoever has my commands and keeps them is the one who loves me. The one who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I too will love them and show myself to them" (John 14:21).

"I am the vine; you are the branches. If you remain in me and I in you, you will bear much fruit; apart from me you can do nothing... If you keep my commands, you will remain in my love, just as I have kept my Father’s commands and remain in his love" (John 15:5, 10).

But the bottom line is, if you obey Jesus, you remain in his love, just as he obeys the Father and remains in his love. This is the example of obedience that Jesus set for us.

Total Commitment

Total commitment is a mainstay of Jesus' teachings. He talks about this a lot in his teachings. This is the thing that is left out of most modern-day gospel messages.

Most Christians stress belief and repentance, which are very important as they are the first steps. They really stress a sinner's prayer, which is good, although it is not in the New Testament; baptism was used to show conversion. But they don't teach about total commitment.

Jesus speaks of a parable, "The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hidden in a field, which a man found and covered up. Then in his joy he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field. Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a merchant in search of fine pearls, who, on finding one pearl of great value, went and sold all that he had and bought it" (Matthew 13:44-46).

He sold everything he owned. It's comparing the kingdom of God to treasures on earth. So, a person might end up selling everything they have to serve God, to do what he wants them to do. But it's saying that that is so much more valuable than the treasures here on earth that it is not even worth comparison. The person in the parable recognizes the extreme value of the treasure and the great pearl, so much that they immediately sell everything to get it.

Then Jesus said to the disciples, “If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me. For whoever would save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for my sake will find it" (Matthew 16:24-25).

And this is true because we have these desires that we want to do, and they're not necessarily the desires that God has for us or the plan that God has for us. There is a plan for us individually, and it says in Ephesians that there are good works prepared in advance for us to do.

He's given us all the talents, all the things that we need to do that plan. So, if you give up your life, you actually find your true purpose in life, the one that will be much more fulfilling than your own plan.

You can do whatever you want. You can choose not to do that. You can choose to live life for yourself. You can have selfish ambition and pleasures and other things, and most people do choose their own way. But in the end, you're going to lose your life. You're going to lose eternal life, and you're going to lose the plan that you had, the purpose that you had, that makes life truly fulfilling.

Jesus told him, “If you want to be perfect, go, sell your possessions and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me” (Matthew 19:21).

Now this is about the rich young ruler. His heart was about money. That's where his treasure was. Jesus knew this. At the end of the story, he walks away, sad because he values the treasures here on earth more than following Jesus. He's not willing to give up those treasures.

But in this situation, and this is most people, they're not willing to give up the things of earth. It might be wealth, family, pleasure, duty, or any other number of reasons. There are many things that become gods to people and stop them from entering the Kingdom of God.

"No one who puts a hand to the plow and looks back is fit for service in the kingdom of God” (Luke 9:62).

When you plow with oxen or horses, you have to look at a point, a tree, a rock, or something like that. You look at that point, and you go straight to that point with the team of horses. If you don't, and you're looking around, not paying attention, looking back, the rows get off, and they become useless. You cannot plant in a field like that. So, then somebody else has to come back and redo it.

It's not talking about glancing back. It's talking about when you're just continually looking away, not keeping your eye on what is before you. That is what the kingdom of God is like. You have to keep your eyes on Jesus and the Word. You have to keep your eyes on the plan.

You have to be obedient and keep to that. If you're always looking back at the world and you're not paying attention to God, then you're not going to be able to do what God has for you, and somebody else is going to have to come and straighten out the work that you were supposed to do in the first place.

You're not fit for the kingdom of God because you're really not able to do the plan that God has for you.

“If anyone comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters—yes, even their own life—such a person cannot be my disciple. And whoever does not carry their cross and follow me cannot be my disciple" (Luke 14:26-27).

The most sacred thing we have is our family relationships. And Jesus is saying, if you don't love me more than them, then you cannot be my disciple. Now, very few churches teach that; it's probably not read too often, and I doubt if too many Bible studies tackle this one in depth. But, Jesus speaks of it plainly and boldly.

He also talks about counting the cost.

“Suppose one of you wants to build a tower. Won’t you first sit down and estimate the cost to see if you have enough money to complete it? For if you lay the foundation and are not able to finish it, everyone who sees it will ridicule you, saying, ‘This person began to build and wasn’t able to finish.’ Or suppose a king is about to go to war against another king. Won’t he first sit down and consider whether he is able with ten thousand men to oppose the twenty thousand coming against him? If he is not able, he will send a delegation while the other is still a long way off and will ask for terms of peace. In the same way, those of you who do not give up everything you have cannot be my disciples (Luke 14:28-33).

So, he's saying, count the cost. Will you be willing to give up everything you hold dear? The rich young ruler counted the cost as too high.

But if you start following Christ, and you have idols or gods, or if something is more important in your life, and halfway through it you decide this is not worth it, I'm going to quit, it's going to cause derision for you, and it's also going to cause harm to the kingdom of God because you're going to be looked down on as a hypocrite and a person that turns back, and you're going to discourage people.

So, Jesus is saying, count the cost. I met a Chinese pastor in a small group, and before he would baptize people in China, he would say, "Are you willing to give up your job and all your money?" And if they said yes, then he would say, "Are you willing to give up your freedom?"

"Yes." "Are you willing to give up your comfort if you're tortured for Christ?" "Yes." "Are you willing to give up your family for Christ?" "Yes." And then the last question was, "Are you willing to give up your life for Christ?" And if they said yes to all those, then he would baptize them, because in China that was a real thing that actually happened to people in his congregation. He actually had people that lost everything, gone to jail, and been tortured for their faith, and most of them suffered for their Christianity. So, this is what Jesus is saying. He's saying, count it. And this is not going to be an easy walk.

God is not your genie. God is not your Santa Claus. He's not going to come and make you healthy, wealthy, and wise and give you everything you want. You were created to serve him, and it may not be an easy task that he has.

So many treat God like he is their employee. They go to him in prayer and give him their orders instead of going into prayer to get their orders. We are his servants; he is not our servant.

"Very truly I tell you, unless a kernel of wheat falls to the ground and dies, it remains only a single seed. But if it dies, it produces many seeds. Anyone who loves their life will lose it, while anyone who hates their life in this world will keep it for eternal life. Whoever serves me must follow me; and where I am, my servant also will be. My Father will honor the one who serves me" (John 12:24-26).

So, these are the teachings, what Jesus actually said about salvation. The New Testament authors reiterate what Jesus says in these different categories.

I think one of the worst parts of teaching half of the gospel, half of what Jesus taught, when it comes to belief and repentance, is the fact that when people live a half-hearted Christian life, without dying to themselves, the world sees their bad example and hypocrisy; they discourage many people from following Christ because of their lukewarm life.

A lot of them don't realize the other aspects of what Jesus said about salvation. They have been taught that God is going to make them healthy, wealthy, and happy, instead of them being taught that they are supposed to wholeheartedly serve God.

I will leave you with the challenge to study more of what Jesus said about salvation, to apply these teachings to your everyday life. Ask God what he has for you to do each day and to help you follow the plan he has for your life.
